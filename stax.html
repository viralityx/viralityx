Do not claim he has nothing

Stax already runs subscriptions, a reviews app, an ambassador program, and live chat. If you walk in saying "you have no reviews" or "you need subscriptions," he will know you did not look and you lose him. The real gap is sharper and more credible: he is missing the two biggest DTC channels entirely, a real email and SMS lifecycle engine and phone-number capture, and his consumable reorder is left to memory. Lead with that. It is true, it is specific, and it is worth real money.

Who you are talking to

The operator, in one breath

Founder who is the expert

Josh built a shop people trust because he knows exactly what to recommend. His staff reputation is the moat. Sell him systems that clone that expertise online and scale his time, not replace his voice.

Omnichannel, not pure ecom

Physical store plus Shopify. The in-store experience is strong, the online experience is behind it. The wedge: your best salesperson is not online, and your online proof lags your real reputation. Both fixable.

Consumable, reorder-driven line

Protein, creatine, pre-workout, greens. People run out on a schedule. That means recurring revenue is right there if the reorder gets driven instead of hoped for. This is your strongest money angle.

The reframe

Your one-line thesis

"You have built the brand and the loyalty in-store. Online you have the pieces but not the machine, and you are leaving the two channels that drive most DTC revenue, email and SMS, completely on the table. Let us turn a product people run out of every month into recurring revenue that runs itself."

Run of show

Discovery, get these numbers

Questions that unlock the sizing

Roughly how does revenue split between the store and online?
Tells you how much of the machine is greenfield and where the upside sits.
How big is your email list, and are you texting customers at all today?
Confirms the SMS gap and sizes the list you can activate immediately.
When someone signs up on the popup or leaves a full cart, what reaches them right now?
Almost certainly just Shopify defaults. Confirms the lifecycle gap.
What share of orders are on subscription, and how do you remind people to reorder?
Sizes the replenishment and subscription upside, your biggest angle.
How are you collecting reviews after a purchase, and why two review apps?
Do not assume. Ask. Opens the reputation and cleanup work.
Who runs your marketing, and how much of it is you personally?
Founder-run marketing is the buying signal. He wants his time back.
How is the ambassador program going, and who manages it?
Manual, most likely. Opens the activation-automation piece.
Are you running ads, and where does that traffic land?
If he is buying traffic with no lifecycle behind it, the leak is even bigger.
If he pushes

Objection handling

I already have subscriptions and reviews.

You do, and that is exactly why this works. The pieces are there but not wired together, and you are missing SMS entirely. We connect what you have and add the channel that drives the most revenue. You are closer than most, not further.

My staff already knows the products better than any bot.

A hundred percent, and we are not replacing them. We are cloning that expertise for the customer shopping online at 10pm when the store is closed. Same recommendations, your voice, available 24/7.

What does it cost?

Depends on what we build, and I would be guessing until we size what you are leaking. Let us put your list size and revenue to it, then I will show you exactly where the money is and what the investment looks like against it.

I can have someone on my team set up Klaviyo.

Some of it, sure. The question is whether it gets built right, stays compliant, and actually gets finished and maintained. This is all we do, and we own the outcome, not just the install.
